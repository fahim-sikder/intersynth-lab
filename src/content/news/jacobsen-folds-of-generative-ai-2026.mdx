At the InterSynth Lab virtual meetup on 22 October, Benjamin Jacobsen (University of York), PI of an ERC project on synthetic data, gave a talk on how the big AI labs use synthetic data to train models.

His main question was what happens when AI models are recursively trained on synthetic data. Drawing on Deleuze's notion of the fold, he looked at how synthetic data already shapes generative AI today: in training, fine-tuning and alignment, and in problems such as model collapse, where the tails of the distribution are cut off. He also raised a political question: if we don't accept the world as it happens to be sampled, who decides which world AI models should represent?

Francis Lee's full notes from the talk are on his blog: [“Synthetic Data and the Folds of Generative AI,” a talk by Benjamin Jacobsen](https://francislee.org/blog/2026-10-22-synthetic-data-as-folding.html).
